# Playbook: devil's advocate memo for a decision

Use when the owner asks you to challenge a proposal before a board meeting, an investment decision, or a negotiation.

## Input

- The proposal and the decision being asked for.
- Supplied material: memo, model summary, minutes, public sources.
- The owner's current leaning, if they want it tested.

Work only from supplied material and general knowledge. If the material is thin, say what the memo cannot test.

## Output

1. **Decision in one sentence**, as you understand it.
2. **Strongest case against**: three to five points, each with the assumption it attacks and the evidence it rests on.
3. **What would have to be true** for the proposal to work. Mark each item verified, plausible, or unsupported.
4. **Questions for the meeting**: five, ranked by how much the answer would change the decision.
5. **What I could not check**: missing data, figures you could not reconcile, sources not supplied.

Keep it to one page. Neutral tone: argue against the proposal, not against the people.

## Quality bar

A junior analyst's challenge memo the owner would take into the room. Every number either matches the supplied material or is flagged.

## Never

- Use or request insider information, client names, deal files, or another party's confidential material.
- Present the memo as a recommendation to trade, invest, or vote. The decision is the owner's.
- Share it outside this private conversation.
